Research / method / provenance

What is outside the picture?

A body has a position.
So does everything around it.

This archive begins with a comparison between anatomical images published by Andreas Vesalius in 1543 and their adaptations by Thomas Geminus in 1545. It asks what happens to our reading of the body when its surroundings change.

Geminus redrew and rearranged images from Vesalius’s works, translating woodcuts into separate copperplate engravings. Selected landscapes and inscriptions disappear; supports remain; figures from different pages are brought together.

The next act of editing belongs to this project: selecting, cutting and repositioning fragments. Each crop remains connected to its source, so that the act of extraction can be read alongside what it leaves behind.
